Making It Fit Your Business

Choose a system that fits, work out the real cost, and roll it out without waste.

On this page: Why this matters | Key ideas | In our store | SA example | Trade-offs | On Monday | Roadmap | Sources

Why this matters to you

  • No ready-made system fits a two-branch store perfectly.
  • Buying the wrong thing wastes money; so does over-customising.

Key ideas in plain English

  • Configure > customise > build: change your process first, customise only with a strong reason (Laudon and Laudon, 2020).
  • Ways to buy: COTS, SaaS, bespoke, low-code. SaaS is a sensible start.
  • Fit-gap: match your needs against each system's features.
  • Total cost of ownership: the whole cost, not just the subscription.
  • Rollout: direct cutover, parallel, phased or pilot. Pilot is safest.
  • Change management: Lewin (1947), ADKAR (Hiatt, 2006), TAM (Davis, 1989).

A real South African example

  • 88% of 500+ SA SME leaders called software positive for survival; 43% found going online moderately hard (EngineerIT, 2021).
  • A Wits study of 84 SA SMEs found digital tools linked to better performance (Mdhluli, 2024).

Trade-offs and risks

  • Vendor lock-in and customisation debt.
  • Price increases after introductory discounts.
  • Load-shedding, staff resistance and migration errors.

What to do on Monday

  • Write the ten things the system must do.
  • Ask staff which tasks are slow or error-prone.
  • Book two or three vendor demos using real examples.
  • Budget for training, hardware, migration and support, not just the fee.
