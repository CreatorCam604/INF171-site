From Paper and Spreadsheets to Real Information

The store already records hundreds of figures a day. The job is turning them into information you can act on.

On this page: Why this matters | Key ideas | In our store | SA example | Trade-offs | On Monday | Sources

Why this matters to you

  • Slips, order books and WhatsApp capture what happened, not whether you made a profit or are running low.
  • Scattered numbers force you to decide on guesswork, not facts.

Key ideas in plain English

  • Data: raw facts with no context (Laudon and Laudon, 2022).
  • Information: data cleaned and given meaning, answers who, what, where, when.
  • Knowledge: information plus experience, answers how and why, drives the decision.

What it looks like in our store

  • Counter (data): a paper receipt for 10 bags of cement, a WhatsApp note for 20 more.
  • Manager's desk (information): one shared list shows 8 bags here, 142 at the other branch.
  • Owner's decision (knowledge): transfer stock by bakkie before Friday instead of a costly urgent order.

A real South African example

  • Hardware, paint and glass retail fell 5.7% year on year in early 2023 (Stats SA, 2023).
  • Structured point-of-sale cuts stock carrying costs by up to 18% and stock-outs by 25% (SEDA, 2023).

Trade-offs and risks

  • Staff used to carbon-copy slips may feel typing codes slows the counter.
  • Load-shedding needs an offline or manual fallback.
  • Structuring data takes after-hours setup time first.

What to do on Monday

  • Gather all data sources from both branches.
  • List the top 20 fast-movers and check where records conflict.
  • Require verified product codes and phone numbers on every slip.
