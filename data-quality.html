The Cost of Bad Data

Dirty data flaws every decision. Clean data keeps cash liquid instead of trapped in dead stock.

On this page: Why this matters | Key ideas | In our store | SA example | Trade-offs | On Monday | Sources

Why this matters to you

  • If branches record products differently, you buy stock you already own and turn away paying customers.
  • Clean data is not paperwork, it decides whether cash stays liquid or gets stuck.

Key ideas in plain English

Six data quality dimensions (DAMA International, 2017):

  • Accuracy: matches physical reality.
  • Completeness: no missing fields.
  • Consistency: recorded the same way everywhere.
  • Timeliness: captured when it happens.
  • Validity: correct formats, units and values.
  • Uniqueness: one master entry, no duplicates.

What it looks like in our store

  • Accuracy: wrong code rings up R80 sand instead of R350 sealant.
  • Completeness: "Jannie" with no number, so an unpaid invoice cannot be chased.
  • Consistency: "Cement 50kg" vs "50 kg Cement" hides stock from one branch.
  • Timeliness: late delivery notes cause the counter to sell timber already gone.
  • Validity: text in a cost cell breaks the =SUM() and the valuation column.
  • Uniqueness: one contractor stored three ways splits his buying history.

A real South African example

  • SMEs lose 15% to 25% of staff hours chasing and fixing data discrepancies (South African Journal of Information Management).
  • Unstandardised descriptions are the top cause of "phantom stock-outs" in building supplies.

Trade-offs and risks

  • Mandatory codes add a few seconds per sale.
  • Cleaning legacy records takes upfront time outside trading hours.
  • Forcing contact details on tiny cash sales annoys walk-ins and invites fake entries.

What to do on Monday

  • Agree one name format for all products: [Category] - [Brand] - [Size].
  • Merge the notebook and WhatsApp group into one deduplicated customer list.
  • Require signed delivery slips at the counter on return, not at the weekend.
