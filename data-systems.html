From Spreadsheets to a System

A shared database gives both branches one trustworthy set of records instead of clashing Excel files.

On this page: Why this matters | Key ideas | In our store | SA example | Trade-offs | On Monday | Sources

Why this matters to you

  • Two people in one Excel file means overwritten rows and versions out of sync.
  • Nobody knows which stock figure is real.
  • A database gives a single source of truth both branches see live.

Key ideas in plain English

  • Spreadsheet vs database: a database enforces rules, so an invalid sale cannot be saved.
  • ACID: a sale and its stock drop happen together or not at all (Laudon and Laudon, 2022).
  • Master data: one Product Master and one Customer Master.
  • Access control (RBAC): cashiers sell, only the owner changes cost prices.
  • 3-2-1 backups: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 off-site.

What it looks like in our store

  • Today: Stock_Master_2026.xlsx locks, so a second file appears and the two disagree by closing.
  • With a system: selling 10 lengths of timber updates the balance from 14 to 4 instantly.
  • The other branch sees it is below reorder and approves a transfer on the spot.

A real South African example

  • Small trade businesses on unlinked spreadsheets lose 3% to 5% of gross revenue to admin errors and phantom stock-outs (GIBS case studies).
  • Entry-level retail software cuts stock reconciliation time by over 70% (Sage, n.d.; Xero, n.d.a).

Trade-offs and risks

  • Transition needs a few days to clean data and train staff.
  • Cloud software is a monthly subscription, not free.
  • Internet dependence needs offline-capable POS and LTE failover.

What to do on Monday: 5-step migration

  1. Audit and freeze all active spreadsheets.
  2. Build one Product Master: SKU, Description, Category, Unit Cost, Selling Price, Reorder Level.
  3. Do a weekend physical count for verified opening balances.
  4. Set user roles and permissions.
  5. Automate a nightly cloud backup.
